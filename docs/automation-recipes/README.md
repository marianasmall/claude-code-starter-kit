# Automation Recipes

Practical, follow-along guides for everyday automations you can build with Claude Code —
written for people setting up their first assistant workflows, not for developers.
Each recipe is one card: what it does, what you need, the steps, and the prompt to start from.

## The recipes

| # | Recipe | What it gives you |
|---|--------|-------------------|
| 1 | [Daily morning brief](01-morning-brief.md) | Start the day with calendar, priorities, and key emails already summarized |
| 2 | [Weekly budget planner](02-weekly-budget-planner.md) | Turn income and spending into a clear weekly budget with savings targets |
| 3 | [Monthly expenses organizer](03-monthly-expenses-organizer.md) | Sort receipts and transactions by vendor, category, amount, and month |
| 4 | [Weekly content planner](04-weekly-content-planner.md) | Seven days of topics, formats, captions, and deadlines in one plan |
| 5 | [Daily evening brief](05-evening-brief.md) | End the day with completed work, open tasks, and tomorrow's priorities |
| 6 | [Slack thread answerer](06-slack-thread-answerer.md) | Summarize long threads and draft a context-aware reply |
| 7 | [Meeting auto-researcher](07-meeting-auto-researcher.md) | Attendees, companies, news, and talking points before every meeting |
| 8 | [Weekly meeting brief](08-weekly-meeting-brief.md) | Next week's meetings combined into one brief with prep notes |
| 9 | [Sales meeting debrief](09-sales-meeting-debrief.md) | Turn a meeting transcript into outcomes, commitments, and follow-ups |

**Where to start:** recipe 1 if you want the flagship experience (it also sets up the
connections recipes 5, 7, and 8 reuse), or recipe 10 if you want a win in five minutes
with nothing to connect.

*Credit where due: the idea list for the first ten recipes was sparked by a use-case
roundup from [@taki.gpt](https://www.instagram.com/taki.gpt/) on Instagram. All guides
here were written from scratch for this series.*

## How these differ from the primers

The [primers](../) explain *features* (plan mode, worktrees, output styles).
Recipes assemble features into a *finished daily habit* — each one ends with something
that runs on a schedule or a single phrase, not just something you understand.

## Format (every recipe follows it)

1. **What you get** — the output, shown before any setup
2. **What you need** — connected tools, in plain language, with links to the relevant primer
3. **Build it** — numbered steps, copy-paste prompts included
4. **Make it stick** — scheduling it (or the phrase that triggers it)
5. **Variations** — how to bend it to your situation
