# High-End and To-the-Trade Imagery

For pieces sold at the top of the market ($20,000 and up) and to interior designers. At that price the reader is trained: designers specify for a living, and private clients have stood in the showrooms these images compete with. The bar is "reads as an editorial shoot, and the product is exactly what gets delivered."

Contents: 1. Integrity rules · 2. The look · 3. What designers need · 4. Disclosure by channel · 5. Reputation cases

Sources are marked. Anything marked *judgment* is editorial practice, not a published standard: no luxury maker publishes its photography spec.

---

## 1. Integrity rules (the maker's side)

1. **The product is the promise.** Fabric, colour, pile direction, seams, cushion count, pillow arrangement, base and proportions must match what ships. A client who receives a different shade of teal on a $40,000 order has a complaint, a return, or worse. Product lock (product-lock.md) enforces "unchanged". Only the maker can confirm "correct".
2. **Colour truth beats mood.** Never grade the product for atmosphere. Warm or cool the room if you like; the product stays as photographed. Shoot the real piece with a grey card or colour checker in one frame so the colour can be matched later. Screens misrepresent fabric colour, which is why the trade relies on physical swatches (Genesys swatch-card guidance, genesys-uk.com, medium confidence).
3. **Scale stays honest.** A 152-inch piece must read as 152 inches against doors, steps and ceilings. AI rooms often shrink the architecture or inflate the product. Check against a known dimension.
4. **No invented provenance.** Don't imply a real client's home, a named designer's project, a showroom that doesn't exist, or a press feature. A generated room is a setting, not a placement.
5. **Concept renders are labelled.** An image made from a shop drawing, before the piece exists, is a concept. Label it "concept rendering" wherever a client could mistake it for a photo.
6. **Keep the AI record.** Keep the original file, the mask, and the verify line with every delivered image. If a client or platform asks what's real, the answer is on file.

## 2. The look (*judgment*, editorial practice)

| AI default | High-end editorial |
|---|---|
| Golden hour, sun flare, glow on every edge | One soft daylight source, usually overcast or north light; shadows that fall one way |
| Postcard view: mountains, lake, sunset | A calm, believable outlook, often slightly overexposed or out of focus |
| Spectacle architecture (spiral stairs, impossible curves) | Real architecture with visible structure, quiet materials |
| Styled to the edges: plants, bowls, books everywhere | Restraint: two or three objects, chosen; negative space |
| Saturated, glossy, HDR | Muted, true-to-life colour, matte surfaces, natural film tones |
| Wide lens, distorted, tilted verticals | 35–50mm, eye level or slightly below, verticals straight |
| Foreground blur props for "depth" | Depth from the room itself |

**The shot set a trade client expects** (*judgment*): a hero in context; a straight-on catalog view on a plain ground; three-quarter views; detail close-ups of the fabric, seams, welting, base and joinery; a scale shot with a person or known object. **Detail shots must be real photographs.** Craft is what the price pays for, and AI invents stitching.

**Resolution:** 300 dpi at print size is the common print standard. One furniture trade database (NOBB, Norway) asks for at least 3,508 px on the long side, with cut-outs for portals (support.byggtjeneste.no, medium confidence; an example, not a luxury standard). A ChatGPT image (often around 1,500–1,800 px wide) is fine for Instagram and the web, and too small for a printed catalog spread.

## 3. What designers need to specify a piece

Dimensions in context, true fabric colour (backed by a swatch), the fabric name, and whether customer's own material (COM/COL) is offered. Add detail shots of construction. A beautiful room shot without these won't get the piece specified. Source: general trade practice (*judgment*); the swatch point is sourced above.

## 4. Disclosure by channel

| Channel | Rule | Source | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google Shopping / Merchant Center | "All images created using generative AI must contain meta data indicating that the image was AI-generated." "Don't remove embedded metadata tags such as the IPTC DigitalSourceType property." The primary image "must clearly display the exact item being sold"; lifestyle images go in a separate field; no text overlays. | support.google.com/merchants/answer/6324350 (checked 2026-10-25) | High |
| Meta (Instagram, Facebook ads) | Ads made or significantly edited with AI may show an "AI info" label under "About this ad". Minor edits like resizing or colour correction may not. | meta.com/help/artificial-intelligence/355108217670024 | High |
| FTC (US) | No AI-specific rule for product images found. The general deception standard looks at the net impression: an image that misstates colour, size or finish is the exposure. | FTC Policy Statement on Deception | Medium |
| California | AB 2355 covers political ads only. The AI Transparency Act (SB 942) puts duties on large AI providers and platforms, not on advertisers. | Jones Day summary, 2024 | Medium |

**For a maker, this means:** the real product photo is the primary image. AI room scenes go in as lifestyle images, with their metadata intact. `finish.py` writes the IPTC tag, and the master PNG keeps it. Check the current policy before a campaign, because these rules change.

## 5. Reputation cases

- **Valentino, late 2025:** an AI handbag ad drew "cheap", "lazy" and "disturbing" even though it was labelled AI (City A.M., medium confidence). Disclosure didn't protect the brand: the images looked AI.
- **Furniture fair, May 2026:** Dezeen reported a major furniture manufacturer showing products against undisclosed AI artwork, and called for fair-wide AI policies (dezeen.com, 2026-05-28, medium confidence).

For luxury, the damage comes from AI a viewer can see. Keep the product real, the room quiet and believable, and the label on.
